Instructions & Formulae

  1. Attempt all questions. Show all reasoning; unsupported answers score zero.
  2. Take $g = 10\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$ unless stated.
  3. Calculators are not permitted.
Mechanical advantage $\mathrm{MA} = \dfrac{\text{load}}{\text{effort}}$; velocity ratio $\mathrm{VR} = \dfrac{\text{effort displacement}}{\text{load displacement}}$; efficiency $\eta = \dfrac{\mathrm{MA}}{\mathrm{VR}} \times 100\%$.

Problems

Problem 1 [5]

Define mechanical advantage, velocity ratio and efficiency of a machine. Prove that for any real machine $\eta = \mathrm{MA}/\mathrm{VR}$.

Problem 2 [6]

A lever has effort arm $1.2\,\mathrm m$ and load arm $0.2\,\mathrm m$. A load of $600\,\mathrm N$ is raised.

  1. Find the ideal effort and the velocity ratio.
  2. If the actual effort is $140\,\mathrm N$, find MA and $\eta$.

Problem 3 [6]

A single fixed pulley lifts a $50\,\mathrm{kg}$ bucket from a well $8\,\mathrm m$ deep.

  1. What is its VR? What effort is needed ideally?
  2. Explain why its efficiency is always below $100\%$ although $\mathrm{VR}=1$.

Problem 4 [7]

A gun-metal pulley system has $4$ strands supporting the movable block. A load of $800\,\mathrm N$ is lifted.

  1. Find VR and the ideal effort.
  2. If $\eta = 75\%$, find the actual effort and the energy wasted in lifting the load $2\,\mathrm m$.

Problem 5 [7]

An inclined plane of length $5\,\mathrm m$ rises $1\,\mathrm m$. A $200\,\mathrm N$ crate is pushed up at constant speed.

  1. Find VR of the plane.
  2. If friction requires an effort of $60\,\mathrm N$, find MA and efficiency.

Problem 6 [6]

A screw jack has a lever of length $R = 40\,\mathrm{cm}$ and pitch $p = 5\,\mathrm{mm}$. Derive $\mathrm{VR} = 2\pi R/p$ and compute its value.

Problem 7 [8]

Two pulley systems $A$ ($3$ movable pulleys) and $B$ (gun tackle, $n=3$) each lift $900\,\mathrm N$.

  1. Find the VR of each system.
  2. Which needs the smaller ideal effort? Which is more affected by friction in practice? Justify qualitatively.

Problem 8 [7]

A wheel and axle has wheel radius $30\,\mathrm{cm}$ and axle radius $5\,\mathrm{cm}$. An effort of $40\,\mathrm N$ lifts a load $M$.

  1. Find the ideal load.
  2. If the machine is $80\%$ efficient, find $M$ and the work done by the effort in one revolution.

Problem 9 [8]

A block of mass $m$ is pulled up a rough incline (angle $\theta$, coefficient $\mu$) by an effort $E$ parallel to the plane. Show that $$\eta = \frac{\sin\theta}{\sin\theta + \mu\cos\theta}$$ for quasi-static motion, and discuss the limits $\mu \to 0$ and $\theta \to 90^\circ$.

Problem 10 [8]

A differential axle (Chinese capstan) has axle radii $r_1 = 8\,\mathrm{cm}$, $r_2 = 6\,\mathrm{cm}$ and wheel radius $R = 48\,\mathrm{cm}$.

  1. Derive and evaluate the velocity ratio.
  2. Why is such a machine prized for lifting heavy loads despite moving them slowly?

Problem 11 [7]

The efficiency of a machine is observed to fall as the load decreases. Using $\eta = \dfrac{L}{E\cdot \mathrm{VR}}$, explain this in terms of a constant "overhead" effort $e_0$ needed to overcome friction at no load.

Problem 12 [10]

Design problem. You must raise a $2000\,\mathrm N$ engine $1.5\,\mathrm m$ using a maximum sustained effort of $250\,\mathrm N$.

  1. What minimum VR is required if $\eta = 80\%$?
  2. Propose a compound machine (lever + pulleys or jack + plane) achieving this, sketch the force path, and estimate the effort displacement.

Solutions (Outlines)

Solution 1. MA $=L/E$; VR $=d_E/d_L$; $\eta = Ld_L/(Ed_E)=\mathrm{MA}/\mathrm{VR}$.

Solution 2. VR $=6$; ideal $E=100\,\mathrm N$; MA $=600/140\approx 4.29$; $\eta \approx 71.4\%$.

Solution 3. VR $=1$; ideal $E = 500\,\mathrm N$; axle friction and rope weight require extra effort.

Solution 4. VR $=4$, ideal $E=200\,\mathrm N$; actual $E = 200/0.75 \approx 267\,\mathrm N$; wasted $\approx 533\,\mathrm J$ over $2\,\mathrm m$.

Solution 5. VR $=5$; MA $=200/60\approx 3.33$; $\eta \approx 66.7\%$.

Solution 6. One turn: effort moves $2\pi R$, load rises $p$; VR $=2\pi(400)/5 = 160\pi \approx 503$.

Solution 7. (a) VR$_A = 2^3 = 8$; VR$_B = 3$. Smaller ideal effort: $A$ ($112.5\,\mathrm N$ vs $300\,\mathrm N$); $A$ suffers more friction (more moving parts).

Solution 8. VR $=6$, ideal load $240\,\mathrm N$; actual $M = 0.8\times 240/10 = 19.2\,\mathrm{kg}$-equivalent ($192\,\mathrm N$); work $= 40\times 2\pi(0.3)\approx 75.4\,\mathrm J$.

Solution 9. $E = mg(\sin\theta+\mu\cos\theta)$, $E_{\rm ideal} = mg\sin\theta$; ratio gives $\eta$. As $\mu\to 0$, $\eta\to 1$; as $\theta\to 90^\circ$, $\eta \to 1/(1+0)=1$ for a vertical lift with no wedge advantage.

Solution 10. VR $= 2\pi R/(\pi(r_1-r_2)\cdot 2)\times 2 = 2R/(r_1-r_2) = 48$; huge VR gives fine control of heavy loads.

Solution 11. With $E = L/\mathrm{VR} + e_0$, $\eta = L/(L + e_0\mathrm{VR})$, decreasing as $L\to 0$.

Solution 12. Need MA $\ge 8$, so VR $\ge$ MA$/\eta = 10$; e.g. 4-strand tackle (VR 4) after a 3:1 lever gives VR 12; effort displacement $\approx 12\times 1.5 = 18\,\mathrm m$.
